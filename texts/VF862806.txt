At Your Fingertips

by Ruth Tenzer Feldman

1 You can find all kinds of weather information, from today’s clouds over Topeka to next year’s predicted rainfall on the West Coast, by going to the National Weather Service (NWS) at www.weather.gov.

2 The NWS is the federal agency that provides weather, hydrologic (water-related), and climate forecasts and warnings for the nation, including U.S. territories and ocean areas. The agency, an arm of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), has about 4,800 employees in 131 weather forecast offices, 19 river forecast centers, and 6 national centers. The NWS has teams of scientists studying satellite, buoy, radar, and computer information to forecast when and where any given condition will likely occur.

3 The NWS Storm Prediction Center forecasts severe weather up to three days in advance and updates information as severe weather approaches. A weather alert system now warns people about 11 minutes before a tornado strikes—which is about 11 minutes longer than people had 20 years ago!

4 The Climate Prediction Center studies weather patterns lasting months or years. These depend on the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), which is a measure of the surface temperature of the tropical Pacific Ocean. Every month, the center publishes the ENSO Diagnostic Discussion forecasting climate conditions in the nation.

5 NWS information is used for adjusting airline flight routes, protecting property from floods or hurricanes, managing crop harvests, and even stocking up on toilet paper.

6 Each year, Americans get more than 1,584,000 NWS forecasts as well as up to 50,000 potentially life-saving severe weather warnings. So the next time the NWS accurately predicts a clap of thunder—or anything else—be sure to give the agency some appreciative claps of your own.
